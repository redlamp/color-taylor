## Description

> **Note, not listing text.** This is a tool for understanding how RGB and HSB/HSL relate to each other. Lead with that - it is what the plugin is for, and it is what makes the hexagon worth looking at.

    Color Taylor is a color hexagon: red, yellow, green, cyan, blue and
    magenta at the corners, white in the middle. Every color sits somewhere
    inside it, and dragging one vector at a time shows you exactly how RGB,
    HSB and HSL each describe the position you just moved to.

    Change the red channel and watch hue, saturation and brightness all move
    in response. Pull saturation down and watch three RGB values converge.
    The relationships between the models stop being arithmetic you take on
    faith and become something you can see.

    It paints as you go, too. Select a layer, pick a color, and the fill or
    stroke updates live - no Apply button.

    WHAT IS IN IT

    - One hexagon, three notations. Drag a channel and watch the other two
      follow. The relationship between the color models is the point.
    - Sliders for RGB, HSB, HSL and alpha, shown together or separately.
      Each track can render its channel in the context of your current color
      or on its own.
    - A swatch library that persists. Twelve saved slots that grow to
      thirty-six as you fill them, drag to rearrange, and sort by hue,
      saturation, brightness or alpha. Alpha is part of a swatch, so the same
      color at two opacities is two swatches.
    - Recent colors tracked automatically. Drag one into a saved slot to keep
      it.
    - Fill, stroke, or neither - "None" reads the selection without writing
      to it.

    Each drag is its own undo step.

    TRY IT IN A BROWSER

    The same picker runs as a web app, with a few extras the panel leaves out
    - the equations view and a walkthrough of how color models relate:

    https://redlamp.github.io/color-taylor/

    PRIVACY

    Nothing leaves the plugin. There is no network access at all, declared in
    the manifest. Saved and recent colors live in Figma's clientStorage on
    your own machine.

## Data security questionnaire

The publish modal's "Share how your plugin handles data" section. Answers
below are checked against the built bundle (2026-08-18) and appear publicly
on the Community page, so keep them true if the plugin ever gains network
access or a backend.

1. **Backend service** - No, I do not host a backend service.
2. **Network requests** - Does not make any network requests. Verified: no
   `fetch`/`XMLHttpRequest`/`WebSocket`/`sendBeacon`/`@font-face`/external
   `<img>` or `<script src>` in `ui.html`; the only `http` strings are W3C
   XML namespace identifiers. Matches `networkAccess: ["none"]`.
3. **User authentication** - No, does not require or use any.
4. **Stores data read/derived from the plugin API** - Yes, locally. Saved and
   recent swatches plus the panel width live in `figma.clientStorage`, and a
   swatch can hold a color seeded from a selected layer's fill, so it is
   plugin-API-derived data. Never leaves the machine.
5. **Updates** - Solo developer.
