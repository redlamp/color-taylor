Oklch lab, second day. Taylor asked how to make chroma predictable and the day ended with an answer: [[decision-oklch-safe-zone]]. Hold C under the chroma every hue can carry at this L, and hue is free. Built as the Safe zone switch on the lab's Oklch bank; the hexagon becomes a hue dial with it on. Lab work only - not for the app yet.

## Built, all in the lab

- Landmark swatches show the nearest in-gamut color at their hue when pinned, C and L both, red where a value moved; a small hexagon under them places the six on their hue rays as rings.
- Sliders panel: RGB, HSB, HSL, OKLCH and OKLAB groups with the app's Source / Mixed pair and its group toggle; every relevant track carries the red and blue marks (lightest and darkest color at this chroma), the white hue edge, or the wash.
- Hexagon: the Hue graph's top and bottom edges as loops, Oklch L as radius after two other mappings were tried and measured; red and blue marks on the saturation and brightness bars, which are the exact positions.
- Shared components grew small additive props: `overlay`, `blBarMarks`/`satBarMarks` on ColorHexagon, `marks` on HexBar, `min`/`wideStepper`/an `lch` and `oklab` group on ColorSlider, an `id` on HexCanvas.
- `oklchGamut.ts`: `nearestInGamut`, `safeChromaAtL`, `lightnessRangeForCH`, `lightnessBoundsAtC`, `validHueSpans`, all with tests.

## Found

- `useDrag` served a stale callback when React reused a ColorSlider instance for a different channel: a press worked, a drag moved the old channel by the old range. Pre-existing. The hook now reads the latest callback through a ref written in a layout effect; `tests/oklch-lab-slider-drag.spec.ts` fails without it.
- Two lessons about the hexagon, both now in the decision note: the lightest color with a chroma always has a channel at 255 and the darkest a channel at 0, and the field has no lightness axis, so the loops are a graph over it and the bar marks are the map.
