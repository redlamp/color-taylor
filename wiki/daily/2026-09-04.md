## The demo's clock, and what it was spending it on

Taylor asked where 5–15 seconds could come off a 49.5s demo. Reading it by

of the clock, and the rest was framing. `afterAction` was 1300ms against a

Neither is a beat; both are slack.

## Sway, not spring

The ghost's lean was a spring on **one frame** of velocity read off **horizontal
movement alone**. Three faults in one line: it overshot a step by 44% and took a
dozen crossings to settle, so the arrow swung back and forth after every stop; at
one frame's resolution "which way is the hand going" has a twitchy answer; and
the two purely vertical gestures moved a perfectly rigid arrow down a track.

Simulating the discrete spring rather than guessing gave 0.10 / 0.65 over four
frames of smoothing — 3% overshoot, inside half a degree in eleven frames. Taylor
had asked for "springier" first and then corrected it to sway, which is the
opposite direction on damping; the simulation is what made the correction cheap.

Vertical counts for half of horizontal. The arrow's body runs from its point at
(1,1) down to about (5,12), and a body trailing its point swings by the cross
product of those two, which puts vertical at 0.36 — half, because the brief for
this cursor was that it be fun.

## Nothing stops dead

Two separate causes, and only the second was obvious. Travel between targets used
easeInOutQuad, which lands from twice its average speed and does the whole
deceleration in the last quarter; across the tool that is 44px per frame arriving
in under a fifth of a second. Smootherstep has zero *acceleration* at both ends
as well as zero velocity.

The gestures were worse: `sin(pi * t)` is at its **fastest** as it lands. Writing
every path as a function of `smooth(t)` — the plain cubic, with a linear clock so
the shaping applies once — traces the identical path at the same peak speed but
starts and stops at rest. Deliberately not the quintic there, whose steeper
middle would speed up the turn as well as softening the ends.

Measured across a full run: all eighteen movements now end between 0.4 and
1.5px per frame, off peaks of 5 to 45.

## The hue strip looped and the ghost went with it

The sweep was a sine about the starting hue, wrapped into 0–360 to find a height.
Right for the value, wrong for the hand: the frame it crossed an end, the cursor
jumped the length of the control. Putting that gesture first is what made it
routine, because it then started from wherever the user's colour was rather than
always from the landing hue.

It turns once now — out to whichever end has the room, back to the landing hue —
and never leaves the strip. Pinned by a test that measures **pixels per
millisecond**, so a starved frame reads the same as a fast one: a dropped frame
advances the path further and the rate holds, while a teleport covers its
distance in whatever frame it lands in.

## Two bugs that only measuring found

**The tick you came back from stayed half full.** The fills are written two ways
— React sets `scaleX(i < index ? 1 : 0)` as a style prop, and the frame loop
writes the playing one straight to the DOM. Going forward those agree, because
the tick just finished flips 0 to 1 in the prop and React writes over whatever
the loop left. Going *back* they do not: the tick being left was already "not yet
played" as far as the prop is concerned, so React sees no change and writes
nothing. A layout effect now puts every fill back to what the render says on each
index change.

**The phone-layout flake was two faults wearing one assertion.** It had gone
twice in fifteen full runs without leaving a message. Splitting the count into
`ghost` and `tips` is what told them apart:

- With the colour unchanged the walk home is skipped, and nothing let go of the
  hover before the page scrolled to the top — so the tip's channel tooltips swept
  down through the demo's own panel.
- The ghost parked below the fold and walked up into its first target, which on a
  phone means walking over the caption it had just arrived to explain.

The second took three attempts. Deciding the entry edge at mount does not work
because the panel places itself on the first frame; doing it once on the frame
after is not enough either because the layout is still settling. It re-parks
every frame until the script moves it, and takes the driver's own position with
it — or the first leg flies from where the ghost used to be.

## The wheel over the field retires

The deck and the plugin had already turned it off because they own their own
scrolling. The picker followed once it turned out to be a liability: a wheel over
the field is a scroll gesture on a trackpad and in device emulation, so the one
place on the page you most want to look at while scrolling past was the one place
that ate the scroll and changed your colour instead.

A second gain, unasked for: the listener has to be non-passive to
`preventDefault`, and that costs the browser its scrolling fast path over the
hexagon whether or not it fires.

## Selection, scoped to the two panels you drag on

A drag beginning a hair *beside* a control — on a channel label, on a heading, in
the gap between two sliders — ran off dragging a highlight behind it. From the R
label that selected "R G B H S B".

A drag starting *on* a control was always fine, because those handlers
`preventDefault` before the browser begins a selection. That is exactly why
`select-none` on the controls could never have fixed this: **the selection lands
on whatever the drag passes over, not on where it started.** So it is a blanket
rule on `#color-hexagon` and `#picker-layout`, with `input` as the only exception
— which covers the channel steppers and the hex field together. The title and the
equations panel are ordinary text and stay copyable, which was Taylor's call.

## The menu becomes a rail that slides

Four passes, and the first two were wrong: a content-sized panel anchored in the
corner, which is a different object from the sidebar Taylor described. Reverted
rather than edited forward. The end state is in [[decision-settings-sheet]],
amended rather than left to contradict the code.

What is worth carrying forward from the layout work:

- **A fixed inset cannot be level with anything here.** The header sits at 44px,
  77px or 107px from the top depending on how the title row and the plugin banner
  wrap. The hexagon card is the reliable reference — it is the first thing in the
  app's own column and moves with all of it.
- **`bottom-auto` is what lets a fixed panel size to its contents**, and
  `min-h-0` on the scrolling body is what lets it shrink under a cap rather than
  pushing the footer off screen.
- **To move centred content, pad the thing that centres it.** Padding
  `#color-picker-root` itself would have pushed the content 200px further left
  than needed; padding `#app-stage` puts the gap exactly where the rail is.
  Measured at 1400: content 151..1249 over a rail at 1004..1384 becomes 20..988.
- **`button` is a labelable element**, so a row's label with `htmlFor` drives its
  switch natively — the browser forwards the activation, brings focus and
  keyboard behaviour with it, and fires once rather than twice.

Audio starts collapsed: it is the section that unfolds into the synth controls,
257px of the rail's height once it is on, and none of what a menu is usually
opened for. "Keep Menu Open" is the last row in Display and makes the whole thing
non-modal — `modal={false}` plus `disablePointerDismissal`, the scrim not
rendered at all rather than made transparent, because a transparent scrim is
still a full-screen element between the pointer and the app.
