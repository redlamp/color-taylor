## The picker gets its demo's ground: `feature/demo`

Prototyped the onboarding demo in an artifact over the last session and settled the layout and
interaction changes it depends on. Ported those to the app today, off `dev`; the demo itself is
next and is specified in [[plan-picker-demo]].

- Recent, Saved and Hex start closed; a `?` button sits between theme and settings, wired to nothing yet.

  blend droplet. `BlendIcon` moved from `figma/ui/lite` into `src/components` so both hosts share it.
- Impact highlights: `useImpact` diffs the readouts against a snapshot taken at the press, keyed by

## Evening: the demo itself

Built it, from [[handoff-picker-demo]]. `src/demo/` - `drive.ts` (the hands), `steps.ts` (the
script), `DemoCursor.tsx` (the artwork), `DemoRunner.tsx` (the stage and the player) - lazy-loaded
by `ColorPicker` behind the `?` button. Eight Playwright cases in `tests/demo.spec.ts`, whole suite
green at 80.

Two things the handoff had wrong, both caught before writing much:

- **`pointerenter` never reaches React.** React synthesises enter/leave from `pointerover`/
  `pointerout` at the root container, so a non-bubbling `pointerenter` dispatched at a stem is
  dropped. Checked it in the browser first: `pointerenter` moved nothing, `pointerover` with
  `bubbles: true` raised the tooltip. Step one is all hover, so this would have been the whole
  step failing silently.
- **Pointer lock would probably have been granted.** The handoff assumed a synthetic press could
  not get it. Chrome gates `requestPointerLock` on *sticky* activation and the user has just
  clicked `?`, so it likely would - taking the real cursor off screen mid-demo, and then `advance`
  reads a `movementX` a synthetic event does not carry. `beginRelative` now skips the lock for an
  untrusted press; one line in `ColorSlider`.

The prototype's `runDemo` turned out not to be portable at all: it tweens the model directly and
fakes the hold with `source = key`. Only the presentation helpers ported. Every step body is
written against the real controls.

What is not from the spec, decided with Taylor as it was built - see [[decision-demo-caption-in-the-header]]:

- The caption is one panel in the header rather than a bubble at the target. A bubble beside the
  thing being worked covers what the caption just asked you to watch; step three had it sitting on
  the H and S sliders.
- The stepper, the progress and the narration switch live in that panel too, one control surface.
- Back and Next: the step index is the state and the effect is the player, so a jump interrupts the
  running step at its next await and starts the new one. No second code path.
- Narration is wired but dormant behind `NARRATION_READY` until Taylor records the four lines into
  `public/demo/`. A step waits for both its choreography and its line, so the voice sets the pace
  where there is one.
- Pacing gathered into one `DWELL` block in `steps.ts`, and slowed: watching takes longer than doing.

Open for the review: caption copy (step one now says "maps to a color channel", not "to the color
hex", which read as the hex code); whether a step should rewind the colour when you step back, or
keep running from wherever it is (it keeps).

Then, unrelated to the demo: an **Interaction Highlights** switch in Settings > Display, above
Border Color Effects. One gate in `ColorPicker` - every lit thing in the picker derives from `lit`
and `held`, and both come from `effectiveHold`, so nulling it silences the sliders, the halos, the
bars, the badge and the hue fill at once with none of them knowing about the setting. Tenth
`localStorage` key, `color-taylor-highlights`, and it *is* wired to reset-all. `color-taylor-muted`
and `color-taylor-effects` still are not - worth deciding whether that is deliberate.

The switch stops at the impact highlights. A control's own direct feedback stays: the hue ray still
fills on a saturation-marker click, because that is the control answering for itself rather than the
tool showing you what else you moved.

Two more on the demo panel. The progress ticks are tracks with a fill now, so the step being played
reads as a playhead rather than a state; each step declares its `duration` as the sum of the DWELL
entries it actually spends, which keeps it from drifting the way a hand-typed number would. `bring`
is the one thing that total cannot predict - a step that has to scroll runs half a second longer -
so the fill clamps and the tick goes solid when the step really ends.

And the sign-off takes itself down after five seconds and a short fade. Through the driver, so Back
or Skip cancels it, and through a new `linger` rather than `wait`: it is time for a person to read
one line, so `?demospeed` does not get to shorten it.

A long round of review notes from Taylor, in one pass.

**The demo.** Caption at `text-2xl`. A fifth, longer tick on the end of the row, which is the
sign-off's own countdown rather than a step - it runs down over the five seconds before the panel
leaves. The ghost now goes home to the hexagon for the sign-off, stands there while the colour
tweens back under it, then walks off the bottom of the screen and fades on the way, instead of
blinking out over the blend button. And a press leaves an expanding ring: a synthetic click moves
no hardware and makes no sound, so without one the blend toggle just changes on its own while a
cursor happens to be nearby. Rings rather than a star or a sparkle - it is what every screencast
tool draws for a click, so it needs no explaining.

**Leaving no trace.** "Return the picker to the value it started at" turned out to be three
separate leaks, not one. The colour tween restores HSB, but `hsbToRgb(rgbToHsb(rgb))` changes 86.4%
of 8-bit colours, so a colour typed as R=137 came back as something else - the snapshot now carries
the displayed RGB and puts it back after the tween lands, but only when the two actually disagree.
The demo's colours were also being recorded into Recent after their one-second settle, and pushed
onto the undo stack. Both are gated on the demo now.

**Elsewhere.** The Figma banner is fixed to the top rather than sitting in the flow, so dismissing
it no longer moves the whole tool - that reverses the note in `App.tsx`, which had it displacing the
picker on purpose. The channel tooltips are mounted always and crossfade on the halos' timing, so
they ease in rather than pop; the two specs that asserted they were absent now assert opacity. The
SB box lost its overflow clip so the handle can hang over the edge rather than being cut in half at
full saturation. The settings panel reads at the base size, not one step down. Dark is the default
theme now rather than the system's preference, it is the switch's left state, and reset-all lands on
it - along with the border colour effects, which it had not been touching.

More from the review, all of it choreography. Every drag now works a **track rather than a handle**:
the hue strip in the colour editor and the H slider in the bank both take the press on the track at
the marker's own position, so nothing jumps on contact and the ghost never has to aim at a 10px
target. Watching a cursor hunt for a handle teaches nothing, and a near miss reads as a bug.
`updateValue` seeds the same accumulator `beginRelative` would, so the wrapping drag that follows a
track press behaves exactly like one from the handle.

Step one visits four stops rather than all six - first stem, the joint two along, last stem, tip.
The full tour made the same point three times and took nine seconds. Step two gained the hue strip
after the colour box, because saturation and brightness alone never leave the one hue.

A fifth step: the two bars on the hexagon, saturation then brightness, swept slowly. Step three
tells you to watch for the impact highlights; this one is the demonstration. The bars belong to the
hexagon rather than the bank, so the sliders lighting up are unmistakably somewhere else - nothing
on screen is the control being held, and the rule is that a control never lights itself. The ticks
now sit at the top of the button row rather than centred on it.

Two bugs the tests found while the step count was changing:

- **Back and Next computed off the rendered index.** Two presses inside one frame both read the
  same `index` and produced the same target, so the second did nothing - which is what clicking
  through the demo actually looks like. They step relative through the updater now.
- The spec had the step count written down. It reads it off the tick row instead, waiting for the
  lazy panel first: before it mounts there are no ticks, and the "all ticks lit" check was
  comparing zero to zero and reporting the demo finished the instant it was asked for.

The plugin arrow's coaxing was rebuilt too. Two 2px nudges and a long rest was invisible; it
oscillates 3px on a 1.6s loop now, and hover holds it at the far end.
