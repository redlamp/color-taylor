OKLCH day. Taylor asked what it would take to bring OKLCH into the app; the answer turned out to be mostly written already, in [[plan-perceptual-color-in-color-taylor]] from 2026-09-01. Nothing had been built and no issue had been filed. Both now exist: issue #146, branch `lab/oklch`.

## The call

Taylor's read is that OKLCH is the goal rather than OkHSL, because OKLCH is what the web actually writes - CSS Color 4, Tailwind v4's tokens, and `src/index.css` already. That agrees with the plan note's recommendation, which wanted OKLCH as a readout for exactly that reason. The plan's other half, OkHSL as a fourth *editing* model, stays unbuilt and is a separate question.

Scope agreed: an OKLCH slider group in the Color Editor, a section in the Equations panel, and an honest `useColorEffects` - it currently drops the HSB hue straight into an `oklch()` hue slot, which is decorative rather than true. All of it tried in a lab page first, reusing as much of the real app as the lab can take.

## Open question, answered by proceeding

Whether the OKLCH bank drags or only reads. A readout is the cheap tier and has no gamut problem; a draggable C slider is nothing but gamut problem, because the valid chroma range depends on the current L and H and moves as either is dragged. Taylor left it open and said to keep building, so the lab is being built draggable with the limit drawn honestly - falling back to read-only is a subtraction rather than a rewrite.

## Built

- `77a441c` - the Oklab/Oklch conversions in `colorConversions.ts`, with culori as the oracle as usual. 158 unit tests pass. Agreement with culori is much tighter than expected: worst case 6.6e-9 in `l`, bounded at 1e-6, with hue given its own 1e-3 degree bound because low chroma amplifies an angle.
- `2f28629` - [[out-of-gamut-must-be-returned-not-inferred]], the day's real finding. The clamped 8-bit colour is identical on both sides of the gamut boundary, so `oklchToRgb` returns `{ rgb, inGamut }`. Blue's 0.3131 / 0.3132 / 0.3133 all come back `0, 0, 255` and exactly one of them is real.

## Costs noted while reading, for when this reaches the app

- `ColorSlider` is integer-only - no `step`, no decimals. OKLCH needs 0.001 on both L and C.
- A fourth 48px selector moves the Color Editor toolbar's measured 306px minimum to about 354px, which shifts where it has to split to two rows.
- `CHANNEL_NAMES` needs an `oklch` arm. L is now Lightness in a third sense, after HSB's B and HSL's L.
- `linearToSrgb` rounds and clamps, which is why the Ok path got its own return shape instead of a new mode on an old function.

## The adversarial pass, and what it cost

Five agents were set on `src/utils/oklchGamut.ts` with instructions to refute it, one of them specifically tasked with confirming the hypothesis that the author had mis-transcribed a constant and then "fixed" a bug of its own making. That hypothesis is dead: all 34 numeric literals match Ottosson’s `ok_color.h` verbatim, checked by three agents independently.

The deviation from a faithful port is justified — but **not for the reason the file gives**. Its headline argument, that Ottosson’s sector dispatch sits on the wrong side of the blue corner over a 1.7e-4 degree band, is wrong by three orders of magnitude: the exact band is 4.43e-7 degrees and the dispatch is accurate to sub-microdegree. The band the file measured was the gamut dilated by our own `GAMUT_EPSILON`. The real justification is the one it mentions almost in passing, plus a stronger one nobody had noticed: a faithful single-sector port is off by 2.9e-3 of chroma at `l = 0.94, h = 101.2` — 163 degrees from blue — where the three-candidate scheme is exact to 3.6e-16. It earns its place across the whole wheel, not at the corner.

One real defect, being fixed: in the wedge `h` 264.05–264.21, `l` 0.45–0.49 the function discards Ottosson’s cap answer for missing the tolerance by 2.2e-6 and settles for one 5.8e-3 worse — up to 5 byte levels, 2,038 of 30,250 cells in that wedge. A plain bisection beats it there, and legitimately: the in-gamut chromas form a single run in that wedge, which is the condition a bisection needs.

## Two wiki corrections, both from measuring rather than trusting

Both notes were `status/verified` and both were wrong.

- [[srgb-gamut-is-not-star-shaped-in-oklab]] said a naive cusp finder returns `C = 0.288` and renders `0,55,255`. Neither reproduces. Bisection returns `0.2656051` → `rgb(0,49,229)`; `0.288` renders `rgb(0,35,241)`; nothing on that ray renders `0,55,255` at all. The conclusion stands, the figures did not — and they had already been copied into the source file before anyone re-measured them.
- [[out-of-gamut-must-be-returned-not-inferred]], written this morning, said the hazard was *loosening* the 1e-6 epsilon. Backwards. Measured: at `1e-4` the needle is **wider**, not shut; at `1e-8` and at `0` it does not exist at all. Tightening is what silently reverts the blue answer from 0.3132 to 0.2656.

The lesson worth keeping is procedural rather than chromatic: a number written down once and cited twice becomes furniture. The only thing that caught either of these was an agent told to disbelieve the file in front of it.
