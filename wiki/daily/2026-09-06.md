## Spectrum bench: the RGB waves over Channel Surfer's rainbow

Taylor brought two pictures: the exploration's red, green and blue zigzag drawing itself in
(stroke-dashoffset to 0), and Channel Surfer's linear rainbow. Wanted: the picker's RGB, HSB and HSL
sliders in a sidebar, and the graph over the rainbow where the hexagon would be, to show how
saturation and brightness are read off the RGB values - saturation the distance from min to max,
brightness the largest channel, hue the three of them taking turns.

Built as a second lab page, `lab/spectrum.html`, composed the way the cube bench is: the same Color
Editor from the same primitives on `useColorState`, nothing in `src/components` touched.
`FlatSection` moved out of `CubeBench.tsx` into its own file so both benches share it. The rainbow
is `src/lab/linear-rainbow.webp`, copied from channel-surfer's `public/demo/`; it lives under the
lab so the production build never carries it.

The finding that shaped it: the waves depend on nothing but the colour's max and min channel -
[[rgb-waves-are-max-and-min]]. `hsbToRgb` and `hslToRgb` swept across hue are one function of
`(h, max, min)`, and `rgbWaves.test.ts` checks both at every hue. So there is one graph, and the
model tabs only change what the rails are labelled: max is B, max − min over max is S, the midline
is L, with HSL's S over the room to the nearer end.

The graph is a picker. Drag sideways for hue, the top rail for brightness, the bottom rail for
saturation; the rails are `role="slider"` hit areas with arrow keys, and max is stacked over min so
that on a grey, where they meet, the one you take hold of is brightness. A press on a rail only
takes hold - applying the press position would hop the rail by however far off it you pressed.

The draw-in is the exploration's stroke-dashoffset tween, staggered 120 ms a channel, and the dash
comes off once drawn so a resize does not leave the line short. "Draw again" replays it. "Sweep
hue" walks a degree a frame through the raw setter, so it is not an edit, and any real edit stops it.

Readouts sit inside the span, off the caption above and the hue readout below, and step outside
only when the span cannot hold them; they cross to the left when the hue marker reaches the right.

## The lab build script, and a bug it had

`scripts/build-cube-bench.mjs` takes a page name now (`spectrum`; `cube` is the default), and each
page builds in its own folder under `dist-lab`, so one build does not delete the other's file.

While at it: the regex that keeps the theme bootstrap was scanning the page *after* the module JS
had been inlined, and react-dom carries a literal `<script><\/script>` in its code. The match ran
from there to the module's real closing tag and wrote the tail of the module out a second time as a
bogus classic script. Barely visible on the cube bench; with the 1.1 MB rainbow in the tail it
doubled the spectrum file to 3.5 MB. The bootstrap is read from the source page before anything is
inlined now.

Verified in the Browser pane: a click at hue 60 gives yellow; dragging the top rail gives B 74 and
RGB 189/189/0; dragging the bottom rail gives S 74 and RGB 189/189/49; the HSL and Both readouts and
the mobile stack look right; the cube bench still loads. 99 unit tests, typecheck and lint clean.
e2e not run: nothing under `src/components` changed.

**Not verified: the draw-in and the sweep.** Both run on requestAnimationFrame, and both browsers
this session could reach were hidden - the pane reported a 0×0 viewport, the Chrome tab reported
`visibilityState: hidden` - so no frame ever ran. Mechanism is the cube bench's orbit loop and a
plain CSS transition on stroke-dashoffset; still, watch them once in a real, visible window.

## Second pass: level with the cards, a Y column, a slower sweep

Taylor's three asks after the first look. The stage no longer takes the viewport: the grid has no
fixed height, so the row is as tall as the Color Editor and Graph cards, and the stage - now a
rounded, bordered box with the same 12px inset - sits level with them. The page scrolls instead of
the sidebar.

A Y column left of the plot draws the spans themselves, to the waves' own scale: **S** from min to
max, **B** from 0 to max, and **L** from 0 to the midline in HSL and Both. The rails run through the
column, which is what ties a bar's ends to the wave. Taylor's call on the fills, and a good one:
brightness and lightness are greyscale, a black-to-white ramp pinned to the scale so a bar clipped
at its rail ends on that rail's grey; saturation is that same grey pulled out to the colour. The top
of the B bar is the bottom of the S bar - brightness gets you to a grey, saturation takes it from
there. Bars sit in the sliders' order, S then B then L, with their letters in the band
above; the readouts stay on the rails. A press in the column is a press at hue 0, clamped rather
than wrapped.

The sweep was a degree a frame, six seconds a lap: too fast to watch the channels take turns. It is
clocked now, degrees a second from a slider on its own row (3 to 120, default 15, 24 s a lap), with
the unrounded hue kept outside the state so the H field never shows a fraction, and the frame delta
capped so a tab coming back from the background does not leap. An edit that leaves hue where the
sweep put it - saturation, brightness, the rails - rides along; only an edit that moves hue stops it.

## Third pass: type, the draw-in, and the hue wrap

Taylor asked what size the text above the graph was. text-xs, with 11px readouts - against a rule
he has set up in global memory and has had to repeat before: all UI text is text-base, headings
larger, smaller only with a reason you could say out loud. The file was there and was not read
before the UI was built. Everything on the page is text-base now, the title text-lg, and the band
above the plot grew to hold the two lines; the drag hint moved into the Graph card, next to the
controls it explains. The cube bench's caption and its HSB/HSL group labels had the same sizes and
are fixed with it. A project-memory pointer at the global file, so the rule loads in every session
here.

The draw-in and its button are gone: the graph does not need to draw itself.

A hue drag on the graph is relative now, as the hue slider's is: a press jumps to the pressed hue
and the drag follows how far the pointer moves, so running off either edge keeps counting round
through 0. A press in the column is still a press at hue 0.

The channel readout above the graph holds still now - each value padded to three figures in the
mono face, so nothing shifts as digits come and go - and the max/min line sits flush with the
plot's right edge.

Title reads "Red, Green, and Blue across the Hue spectrum": each name in its ink, and the last two
words filled with the hue slider's own gradient.
The gradient on those words runs at full brightness and 80 saturation: pure hues go dark through
the blue third on this ground, and a little white lifts them.

## Fourth pass: a smooth sweep, and channel checkboxes

The sweep hopped. It wrote whole degrees, so at 15°/s the marker moved fifteen times a second and
slower was worse. It writes a fractional hue every frame now; the H field and the hue bar round it
for display, the state is rounded once when the sweep stops, and while it runs the marker takes the
state's hue rather than the one read back off the 8-bit colour, which steps coarsely at low
saturation.

Each channel in the readout above the graph carries a checkbox in its own ink. Off, that channel's
wave and dot leave the graph; the rails stay, since the colour has not changed. The caption is
otherwise pointer-transparent, so the graph beneath it still takes a hue press; only the labels
take the pointer.

The max/min sentence and the drag hint are gone. The three channel readouts now sit over the plot,
each in a wider slot centred on the hue where its channel peaks - red at 0, green at 120, blue at
240 - so each stands above its own band of the spectrum; red's peak is the left edge, so it clamps
to start there rather than hang into the column.

The max, min and max − min readouts floating over the picture were a distraction, Taylor said, and
asked for a compact home in the left-hand column. They live there now: the column gained an axis
strip on its left where the raw max, min and midline values sit on their rails, stepping apart when
the rails close up, and each bar carries its own reading - "S 100%", "B 100%", "L 50%" - written
along the bar and held within the plot's height. The letters above the bars went with them, since
each bar now names itself. Nothing floats over the spectrum but the hue marker and its dots.

Then the bar readings went from along the bars to above them, at 45° like a spreadsheet's angled
column heads: each pill's foot on its bar's left edge, rising to the right over the columns beside
it. The column widened to 208 and the bars spread to 40 apart so the headings clear one another
(parallel at 45°, their spacing across is the gap over root two, and that has to beat a heading's
height), and the band above the plot grew to 120 to hold the longest of them. In Both, S reads
"100%·100%", HSB then HSL, kept short for the same reason.

Sweep hue is a switch row now, like Spectrum and Rails beneath it, with the speed slider tucked
between the label and the switch. Composed from the row's own parts, the panel Label and
SettingsSwitch, since SwitchRow has no middle slot; the label drives the switch through htmlFor as
the shared row does.

## Fifth pass: columns to the right, one header row, L always

Taylor could not see the lightness column - it only showed in HSL and Both. All three bars show
now whichever model the editor is on, with the midline rail and its axis value alongside; only
the S reading still follows the model, since HSB and HSL measure it differently.

The columns moved to the right of the spectrum: the axis strip beside the plot with the rails'
values, the bars beyond it, the angled headings still rising to the right into the room the
column keeps for them. A press past the plot's right edge is a press at hue 360.

The band above the plot holds two rows that together match the band the headings rise through:
the title, and under it the channel checkboxes back over their hue peaks, flush to the top of the
spectrum. (A first cut put title and checkboxes on one line, as a group at the right; Taylor
wanted the peaks kept, and the two rows.)
With the plot starting at the stage's left edge, the marker and its readout at hue 0 sat half off
the stage; a 12px left inset gives the dots room, and the readout is held inside the stage at
both ends of the circle. The checkbox slots narrowed to 80 so the group clears the title at the
width the bench is usually seen at.

The band above the plot had been sized for Both's long S heading, so in HSB and HSL there was
56px of nothing above the title. It is sized to the mode now - 84 for HSB and HSL, where the title
sits 20 from the top, 112 only in Both, where the long heading fills the height. At 45° a heading
of width w and height h rises (w + h) over root two: 68 for "S 100%", 98 for the Both one.
The axis values round to whole numbers: they are 8-bit levels, and a midline of 127.5 is not one.
The axis values are right-aligned, and the title is text-2xl.

Ctrl-click (Cmd on a Mac) on a channel's checkbox solos it - the other two leave the graph - and a
second Ctrl-click on the soloed one brings them back, the way a mixer's solo releases. The words
take a modified click too; the label's own activation would forward a plain click to the box, so
it is stopped there. React drives a checkbox's change from its click, which is what lets the
change handler read the modifier keys off the native event.

A hue drag on the graph snaps to the six corners - red, yellow, green, cyan, blue, magenta -
within three degrees or four pixels, whichever is the smaller at the plot's width (about two
degrees at the usual width; five was too grabby, two-or-two too slight). The drag's own position is never snapped, only what it emits, so it slides
off a corner as freely as it settled on it. The sweep and the sidebar's hue slider are untouched.

## The sweep stopped under a saturation drag

Taylor asked that the sweep keep going while saturation and brightness are adjusted - which was
the design, and it did not hold. The stop rule compared an edit's hue with the hue the sweep last
wrote, and that reference moves under a slider drag: a drag's update can be applied after the next
frame has already advanced it, so an innocent saturation edit read as a hue edit. The rule is
deterministic now - only the controls that name a hue stop the sweep: the hue sliders, a hue press
on the graph, the hex field and the RGB channels. Saturation, brightness, lightness and the rails
ride along.

The HSL S and L sliders needed one more thing. An HSL write rebuilds the colour from a hue frozen
at the start of the gesture ([[decision-hsl-gesture-origin]]), which under a sweep would drag hue
back to that origin on every move. While the sweep runs they are set through the hue of the
moment instead, changing only s and b; off the sweep they go through the gesture origin as before.
Found while checking: an arrow key on a rail grip bubbled to the stage's own key handler, which
read it as a hue step - so a keyboard nudge of saturation also turned the hue by a degree, and
under the new rule stopped the sweep. The stage now takes keys only when it is the target.

The page is capped at 1440 and centred, so the spectrum stops growing with the window: at the cap
the plot is about 770 wide, a little over two pixels a degree. The whole grid rather than the
stage alone, so the graph and the editor stay side by side inside it.

## Color Editor cleanup and the Swatches panel

Taylor came with four notes on the Color Editor, drafted on a design canvas first: the hex field up
onto the toggle row at the right, stepper-wide; the "Hex and HTML Colors" card gone and its row flat
under a rule; the second swatch gone with it. Then the question of Recent and Saved, which turned

plus component - because the app now renders the lists in a different panel from the wheel while
the plugin still wants them under it. The hexagon keeps one callback, `onRecordColor`, for the
clicks that record a colour at once. The bank is a parameter now: 24 in the app, 12 in the plugin.
Tests that reached into `#hex-group` or read Sort from `#saved-colors` moved with the controls.

Then a run of follow-ups: "Sure?" in the destructive tint, the Show / Hide pair replaced by one tags
pill, a fifth demo step that presses it, the tour visiting the red handle, the green stem and the
