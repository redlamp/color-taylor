## The deck's hexagon

Reported: on `12-hexagon` the blue stem draws over the green handle and green over red, and the
handle fills do not match what is under them.

- **Stack order fixed** (83ffd0f). The chain was drawn interleaved - stem then its own handle, per
  channel. `ColorHexagon` draws all stems then all handles, which is why it cannot show this.
- **The fills are not a layering problem.** The deck's wheel is CSS - a conic gradient of
  `hsl(H,100%,50%)` under a white radial, then a black overlay for brightness - while the fills come
  from `hsbToRgb(h, s, brightness)`. Two colour models with no shared code. In the app both come
  from `colorAtPoint`, which is why the staple holds there.
- **Also found:** the selected dot sits at `(S/100) x edge` while the stems end at chroma. They
  coincide only at B=100; at B=40 the gap is 84px on a 140px radius. Independently the same bug
  class as the `r^2` one fixed in the app yesterday.
- **Decided** ([[decision-intro-renders-the-real-picker]], #81): the app is the source of truth and
  the deck should render its components. It already does - `ColorSlider`, `EquationsPanel`,
  `ColorPicker`. `HsbCircle` was the lone exception and the lone thing that drifted. Open question
  is the circle-to-hexagon morph, which is the only thing `HsbCircle` uniquely provides.

## The morph, and the last second renderer

The open question from [[decision-intro-renders-the-real-picker]] closed the same day it opened,
and in the direction the note leaned away from.

- **`ColorHexagon` learned to be a circle.** `shapeEdgeDist(angle, r, mix)` - the shapes differ in
  exactly one number, whether the edge distance varies with angle, so lerping that is the whole
  morph. One `shapeMix` prop drives the shader edge, the outline, the brightness cross-section, the
  handle fills and the pointer mapping together, so it is a real shape at every frame and stays
  draggable part-way through.
- **`HsbCircle.tsx` is deleted.** It existed for the one thing the real component could not do.
  There is no second renderer left in the deck.
- **The feared cost did not materialise.** Option 3 was rejected in the morning for putting a
  presentation-only concern into the app. The interpolated edge is not one - it is the app's own
  geometry making the deck's argument. `shapeMix` defaults to 1; picker and plugin unchanged.
- **Fell out of it:** the shader edge was a hard `discard` with `antialias: false`. A straight
  hexagon edge hides its own stair-stepping; a slowly curving one mid-morph does not. Feathered over
  a device pixel, which improves the app too.
- `tests/intro-shape-morph.spec.ts` measures the outline's longest-to-shortest vertex radius - 1 a
  circle, `2/sqrt(3)` a hexagon - and asserts a sample strictly between the two during the
  transition, which is what separates a morph from a cut. 55 e2e green, figma build clean.

## The wheel keeps the handle, loses the stems

Follow-on from the morph. The wheel showed the full RGB chain, which it has no business showing:
there is no geometry on a wheel that corresponds to the channels, so three segments claiming *this
much red, then this much green* are an explanation the shape cannot support.

- `chainReveal` on `ColorHexagon`, 0 to 1. The stems, the joints and the origin fade; the handle at
  the end never does, because it is the selected colour and every picker shows that.
- Tied to `shapeMix` in the deck rather than given its own timing - they are the same claim. The
  stems arrive exactly as the hexagon that earns them does.
- Faded stems are not draggable. An invisible hit target is worse than no target, and the wheel
  would otherwise have carried three of them across a field showing no lines.

**Open:** the tip handle's ring is the last channel's colour, so on the wheel it reads as a blue
loop on an orange colour with nothing to explain the blue. Correct once the stems arrive; arbitrary
before. Left as-is pending a call.
