A day on the hexagon's brightness bar that turned into a two-column layout change. All on `fix/plugin-swatch-grid`, which by now carries far more than its name.

## The brightness markers were dead, not just small

Asked to make `100` / `50` / `0` clickable and to label the bar. The labels turned out never to have worked at all: they are HTML over the SVG, and `#hex-svg` sits at `z-[5]`. A root `<svg>` is not a shape — it takes the hit over its whole box — so all three sat underneath it and never fired, never even lit up on hover. No throw, no log, and the cursor still turned into a pointer. The only way to catch that is to assert on the resulting value, which is what the new spec does.

`z-[6]`, hit areas from 5×10px up to 13–24×18px, and a transparent band across the tick gutter because a 4×1-unit tick is a target in name only. Write-up, including the residual ±3 dead zone under the value pill: [[decision-brightness-axis-not-luminance]].

## "Luminance" was the wrong word

Asked whether it was correct. It was not, and the repo's own math says so: B is `max(R,G,B)`, L is `(max+min)/2`, and luminance is a weighted sum of *linear* RGB. Blue and yellow sit at the same point on the bar and differ 13× in actual luminance. HSB and HSV are also one model — B and V are the same axis, which the `rgbToHsb` docstring already admitted.

Caption dropped on the user's call; the bar names its own axis now. Three placements before one cleared both the value pill and the `R` channel letter, and two mechanics worth remembering are in the note: `vertical-rl` + `rotate-180` over `rotate(-90deg)`, and Tailwind v4's `px`/`py` being *logical* properties, which puts `py-1` on the wrong axis in vertical writing mode.

## Removing 10px of caption broke the columns

The interesting part. `index.css` said the hexagon needs no slack absorber because it is the taller column and therefore sets the height the other matches. Dropping the caption took it from 773 to 756, the sliders column became the taller one, and a start-aligned hexagon cannot be matched downward — it sat short with the row open beneath it. 17px at 1500px, **128px at 900px**, because the hexagon's height follows its width while the slider rows do not.

My first fix was to let the hexagon stretch. Bottoms levelled, and the 128px moved *inside* the card — which is precisely the mostly-empty card the rule I had just weakened existed to prevent. I reported that fix as done having measured only 1400 and 1500px. Too narrow a check for a rule about window-dependent layout, and the user caught the consequence by asking.

The real answer came out of the user's own proposal: give both columns a permanent absorber and the conditional mechanism disappears. `#hex-stage` grows on the left with the wheel centring in it; `#sb-wrapper` grows on the right. The `absorbs` prop, the `data-section-grow` attribute and the CSS exemption are all deleted. [[decision-both-columns-absorb-slack]].

Also promoted the SB box out of its card so the absorber cannot be closed, which let "Color Editor" move up to the panel it actually describes — [[decision-color-editor-is-the-panel]]. `bun run test` is a width sweep now, and it fails without the fix, which I checked.

## Vetting paid for itself

The user asked for the restructure to be vetted before building. Reading it against the code turned up that the Color Editor section was the column's only absorber, that `min-h-24` was documented as what kept the columns flush, and that parts 1 and 2 were inseparable — renaming the panel while the inner card kept its name gives "Color Editor" containing "Color Editor". Measuring the slack across widths *before* touching anything is what produced the 128px number, which reframed the whole thing from polish to bug fix.
