## The Oklab research session

Long conversational dig through perceptual colour, prompted by "how hard would OkHSV be?". Written
up as [[color-models]], a new MOC, plus four notes. No code, no issue filed.

- [[oklab-and-the-perceptual-color-spaces]] - the survey. CIELAB and why ΔE2000 exists, what "Ok"
  means (Ottosson's own joke: *an OK Lab*), the real criticisms, and the successors - Oklrab,
  CAM16-UCS, HCT, Jzazbz, ICtCp. With sources.
- [[constant-lightness-needs-the-hsl-family]] - **the finding worth keeping.** Holding perceived
  brightness flat while rotating hue is an HSV-vs-HSL question, not sRGB-vs-Oklab. `V` means "how far
  to *this hue's* ceiling" and the ceilings differ (yellow L=0.968, blue L=0.452), so no V-based
  model can do it in any space. OkHSL measures **spread 0.000**. HSB and HSL both 0.516.
- [[srgb-gamut-is-not-star-shaped-in-oklab]] - at blue's own lightness the in-gamut chroma runs are
  `[0..0.2658]` and `[0.3131..0.3133]`. 0.0006 deep, invisible to a person, and it made my cusp
  search render pure blue as `0,55,255`. Trace the cube's edges instead of searching.
- [[plan-perceptual-color-in-color-taylor]] - the recommendation: OkHSL as a fourth mode, Oklch as a
  readout, P3 as a drawn boundary rather than a mode, and keep `blMode` at two values so its 30
  two-way branches don't become 30 four-way ones.

**Corrected mid-session:** I first recommended Oklch *over* the OkHSL/OkHSV pair, having lumped them
together. OkHSV's normalisation buys only comfort; OkHSL's buys constant lightness, which is the
lesson itself. Also claimed an OkHSV hue sweep looked flatter in lightness than HSV's - it does not,
both strips hold the identical set of colours. Measurement caught both.

**Also proposed:** a deck slide after `12-hexagon` - the hexagon's own pixels relocated to where
Oklab puts them. Noted on [[hexagon-is-the-cube-down-its-diagonal]].

## The circle in the main app, and what it does to the stems

Asked whether the intro's circle could come into the picker, and whether the RGB chain could map with
it. Assessed, not built - [[rgb-stems-must-curve-in-circle-space]], with a playable prototype at
`docs/prototypes/rgb-stems-circle-space.html`.

- **The legs are forced to bow.** The hexagon is a *linear* projection of the cube, which is the only
  reason three straight legs add up to the colour. Hexagon to circle is a radial stretch of
  1.0 at the vertices to 1.155 at the edge midpoints - angle-dependent, so not affine, so the sum of
  the warped vectors is not the warp of the sum. Warping the joints and curving the legs is the only
  option that keeps a leg meaning *add this much of this channel*.
- **Chords are close and wrong.** Points along a chord do not correspond to intermediate channel
  values, so leg hover and leg drag would select from a curve that is not drawn.
- **The first leg never bends** - it runs from the centre along a channel axis, and all three axes
  point at vertices where the stretch is exactly 1.
- **The app's circle must mean chroma, not saturation.** The deck's wheel ignores brightness on
  purpose; under that reading the chain needs a `1/b` scale that diverges toward black.
- So `shapeMix` has to become two props: the boundary shape, and the radial semantics. The deck wants
  them coupled; the app wants only the first.
- **Found while checking:** three callsites still use hexagon-only geometry, including the
  click-to-select hit test at `ColorHexagon.tsx:1694`. The deck never exercises them because it runs
  `wheelAdjusts={false}`.

## Keyboard control, decided

Reviewed the project as a whole and named three things to do first; the third was keyboard access for
the hexagon (`tabIndex` 0, `onKeyDown` 0, `role="img"`). Taylor's scheme, from a gaming background:
WASD on the hexagon, Tab between cards. [[decision-keyboard-scheme]], filed as #84.

- **The rule:** arrows move the bars, letters turn the wheel. Arrows adjust along the slider they point
  along on every card; the hexagon's bars are vertical and horizontal, so ↑↓ and ←→ each get one.
  Not a special case - which is what makes it one line in the `?` overlay.
- **What makes it safe:** `E A D F` are hex digits. The letters live on the hexagon's own `onKeyDown`,
  never `window`, so focus scopes them by construction and typing `#ADDFEE` cannot fire them.
- **Nothing to disable:** the only globals are `Ctrl/Cmd+Z/Y`, modifier-gated.
- Polar arrows were the first proposal and were dropped for arrows-duplicate-WASD, because the arrows
  should point along the thing they move.
