## Shipped

`fix/intro-tween` → `dev` → `main`, CI green both times, deploy verified. Five commits, one concern each:

- The deck's keyframe animation was **crashing, not freezing** — an undefined keyframe threw inside the rAF callback, before the reschedule, so the loop died and stayed dead for the rest of the deck. Worst case measured: 21 seconds pinned on one colour across six slides. See the commit for the clock mix that caused it.
- Thousands and millions merged; they rendered the identical grid from the same generator.
- The Settings panel no longer shows over the last slide. It is `position: fixed` and hides by sliding off the viewport, but the last slide mounts the app inside a `transform: scale()` wrapper — and a transformed ancestor becomes the containing block for fixed children, so "off the viewport" became "off the wrapper". The capturing transform was `matrix(1,0,0,1,0,0)`; the identity still establishes a containing block.
- Direct input now pre-empts a colour tween instead of losing to it. Not hex-specific — no handler cancelled the tween, so dragging a slider mid-tween lost its input too.
- ~640 lines of unreachable deck code deleted, six files removed.

Also: H matches S and B as a linear gradient, subtitle is "UX and Product Designer", and the ramps slide carries both dates — the jump from thousands to millions took a single OS release.

## The cube

Built [[hexagon-is-the-cube-down-its-diagonal]] as an interactive prototype: `docs/prototypes/rgb-cube/`. three.js vendored beside it so it opens by double-click; nothing added to `package.json`.

The idea earns its place because it is provable from the app's own constants rather than being an interpretation. What fell out of building it, which I had not expected: **black and white both collapse to the centre** under the projection, which is the honest answer to why brightness needs a separate control.

Three mistakes worth remembering:

1. **Perspective camera.** A cube down its diagonal is only a hexagon under a *parallel* projection. The first version's central claim was visually false.
2. **`material.clippingPlanes` silently does nothing on a raw `ShaderMaterial`** — it needs three's clipping GLSL chunks. One `discard` in the fragment shader replaced it.
3. **Built the page from a byte range that cut off before `</style>`**, so the whole document was swallowed as CSS and rendered blank. The file looked fine on disk. The assembler asserts the style block is balanced now.

Teaching plan and proposed script rewrites: [[plan-teaching-rgb-to-hsb]].
